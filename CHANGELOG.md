# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

Nothing is tagged or published yet. Everything below can still change without a migration note, and the JSON contract is not stable until there is a `v0.1.0`.

**Breaking**

- `excerpt.rendered` is gone. It was `content`, byte for byte: equal in all 13 articles and all 26 leden of BWBR0041459, and 14% of the answer for nothing. Read `content`.
- `excerpt.text` is now optional and travels only with `--raw`. Without it an excerpt carries `start` and `end` and nothing else, so no markup crosses the wire for an answer nobody will read it in. An answer that does carry it also carries the `unverified_slice` caveat: the slice is the service's word for what stands at those offsets, not a cut bron made from bytes it hashed.
- `provenance` says who froze the bytes, as `frozen_by: "bron"` or `frozen_by: "service"`. `bron` carries `fetched_at` and is what `case` and `publication` answer with, because bron fetched those bytes itself. `service` carries no moment and is what the wetgeving lane answers with: the service does not say when it froze a document, deliberately, because a reader who can see when something was fetched can see what is being worked on. The two are different claims about what a span supports, and asking whether an unrelated timestamp happens to be present was not a way to tell them apart.
- `bron law show --refresh` is gone. It asked whether the corpus was still the whole story; this lane has no corpus to ask about.

**Added**

- `bron law`: search by any name the register gives a regulation, show a law or one article, list every published version, and diff a law between two dates. Articles are matched on upstream's own identity, so a renumbered article reads as one amendment rather than a removal plus an addition.
- `bron find`, which answers which article, from a question or from a citation. A question is ranked over the shared corpus by a vector; a term that reads as a citation is looked up instead, because `3:305a` put through a vector comes back as the Wft's numerically similar articles and never as artikel 3:305a BW. The two answers stay apart: `data.kind` is `ranking` or `lookup`, the word the caveats and the failures already use for the value a consumer switches on, a hit carries the `distance` that ordered it and a resolved citation carries none, and a citation that reads more than one way is answered every way with a `several_readings` caveat rather than bron picking one. A reading that came to nothing says why. A citation that resolves to nothing is a not-found, never an empty ranking in its place.
- `--as-of` and `--as-known-on` as separate flags. Geldigheid says what applied, zicht says what the register showed, and they diverge whenever something is published with retroactive effect. Both resolve once at startup so a run cannot straddle midnight.
- `--raw` on `bron law show` and `bron law changes`, which asks for the publisher's own bytes at every span. An answer that carries one says so with the `unverified_slice` caveat, read off what is in the envelope rather than off the flag: `--all` and `--paragraph` both change which excerpts travel.
- `bron case`: search decisions, fetch one by ECLI, and ask what its own relations say about appeal or cassation. `no_published_successor` is a claim about what the register held at the moment bron looked, dated to it, and not about whether an appeal was brought.
- `bron publication`: search and fetch kamerstukken, Staatsblad, Staatscourant and Tractatenblad.
- `bron archive`: list captures, show a page as it stood on a date, and submit a page for capture. Answers are graded `archive` and dated to the capture, never to today. Capture is the only command that writes to a third party and the only one needing credentials.
- Content-addressed provenance. Every document is named by the SHA-256 of its own bytes, every answer carries the digest of the one it rests on beside the address it came from, and articles and leden carry byte ranges into it. A reader checks a quotation by fetching that address and hashing what arrives; for an archive capture, without following redirects (`curl --max-redirs 0`), because the archive replays a captured 301 as a real 301 and a client that follows it hashes a different capture.
- `--json` on every command, in a versioned envelope carrying a `grade` that separates a statute or ruling speaking with its own force from an archive's record of what a page said on a day.
- Exit-code contract: 0 ok, 1 usage, 2 not found, 3 network, 4 upstream, 5 local. A command line the parser will not take exits 1 like any other usage failure, rather than 2 with the argument parser's own code, because 2 means not-found here. On failure with `--json` the error is still JSON on stdout, with the human sentence on stderr.
- Per-host rate limiting coordinated through a file lock, so processes sharing a machine share one budget. Any language that can take an `flock(2)` can join it; see [docs/buckets.md](docs/buckets.md).
- `BRON_HOME` to relocate the rate-limit buckets, which is the only thing bron writes.

**Changed**

- There is no local corpus. `src/db.rs`, `src/schema.sql`, the SQLite dependency and the commands that existed for them, `bron update`, `bron verify`, `bron db path` and `bron db stats`, are gone, along with the BWB manifest and version-XML readers that filled it. bron writes one thing now: its share of the per-host rate-limit budget under `BRON_HOME`. The accepted consequence is that without a network bron does nothing. It never answered offline for the legislation lane anyway, since the corpus only held what someone had already fetched, and it now holds nothing.
- `resolved_via` is gone. It existed while one lane could be answered two ways and it has said `mirror` and nothing else since the publisher route went. Where an answer rests on bytes, `provenance.frozen_by` already says who froze them, per document, which is the same fact stated once instead of twice; where it does not, as in a ranking, there is no second source it could have come from.
- `--direct` is gone and `BRON_MIRROR` is no longer optional. Both turned the service off, and there is nothing left to turn it off in favour of. A value that names no service is refused rather than read as silence: `""`, `"/"`, `"/api/v1"`, `"example.com"` and `"/some/path"` all fail on one rule, an address with a scheme and a host, and at exit 1. Two of those used to reach the first request and come back as `upstream`, which charged the service for what the caller typed.
- A reading of a citation says what the service holds for it as one field rather than a flag beside three that are only there when it is set. `held` is `text` with the `content_hash` a consumer resolves, or `none`. Where the service disagrees with itself, in either direction, the reading is answered as `none` and carries a `reading_contradicted` caveat naming it: `none` is the half that claims least, and a caveat on the reading leaves the citation's other readings standing where dropping the answer would take a sound one with a broken one.
- `kind` 5 is `local`, not `corpus`. There is no corpus here to be unusable. What exits 5 is bron failing at its own end: an HTTP client that would not build, a rate-limit directory that cannot be found, a stdout that will not take the answer. A `corpus` failure from the service arrives as `upstream`, as every other failure it reports does.

- `law search`, `law show`, `law versions` and `law changes` read through the bron service rather than resolving dates against a corpus of their own. Which rendition a date resolves to is one rule, and bron implemented it twice: since the service began excluding renditions the publisher has withdrawn, the CLI went on picking them, asking the publisher for text that is no longer there and ending in a redirect loop. That reached 140 renditions across 108 regulations. `bron law show BWBR0008691 --article 1 --as-of 2018-01-01` failed and now answers from `2016-07-01_0`, saying which rendition was withdrawn.
- `law search` returns more. The service matches on the abbreviations and citeertitels in the register's own WTI rather than on SRU titles, so `Awb` finds the Algemene wet bestuursrecht. Its window is 20 by default and 100 at most, and a cut list says so with a `truncated` caveat.
- `held` in a `law versions` row is now whether the service holds that rendition's text, not whether this machine does.
- `law show` answers `not_found` for a regulation whose text the service does not hold. The rendition and its dates still resolved; what is missing is the text, and the message names the rendition so it can still be fetched from the publisher.
- An article the register publishes without a `stam-id` is not in the answer. The service keys articles on the publisher's own identity and holds none without one, which is what makes a comparison an identity match rather than a guess. It costs the annexes: the Awb answers with 570 articles where parsing its own XML yields 584, and the 14 missing are Bijlage 2 and Bijlage 3, the Bevoegdheidsregeling bestuursrechtspraak and the griffierecht tariffs. Measured against BWBR0005289, which loses none.
- Three caveats state what the service resolved: `withdrawn_renditions` names renditions the publisher pulled that cover the date, `ambiguous_rendition` names published ones that also cover it, and `superseded_knowledge` says the register has since revised what it shows for that date. `windows_disagree` is gone, because bron no longer resolves the two windows itself.
- `upstream_digest_differs` no longer carries the two digests. The comparison is the service's, made against bytes bron does not hold, and printing hashes here would read as bron having hashed something.
- A limit the service states and this bron has no name for is reported as `unrecognised` under the service's own name, rather than dropped.
- An answer from the service is no longer written to the blob store. Nothing points into it, no ledger row names it, and what it does point at is a blob already addressed by the hash of itself. `bron find` was the last one still frozen, and it is the one that matters most: the question travels in a POST body precisely so it is not written down, and the answer to it is that question's ranking with a timestamp beside it.
- `bron case`, `bron publication` and `bron archive` fetch, parse and render, and write nothing. They read the publisher directly and will keep doing so: none has an API equivalent, and the archive lane never will, because the Internet Archive rate-limits by address and a service making captures would put every user on one budget. What they no longer do is store the answer in SQLite and read it back to build it, which was the last thing writing to a store that is going away. Nor do they keep the bytes: the digest of what was read travels with the answer, beside the address it came from, and a reader checks a quotation by fetching that address and hashing what arrives. For an archive capture, fetch it without following redirects (`curl --max-redirs 0`): the archive replays a captured 301 as a real 301, so a client that follows it hashes a different capture. Keeping an unindexed copy of every fetch in a store that is deliberately never evicted is not what that store is for.
- `bron case appeal` answers from the decision's own relations, so it answers for a decision bron has never fetched before instead of reporting that it holds nothing. It costs a request to do so: where it used to answer from disk with exit 0, it can now exit 3 or 4 when the register is unreachable or unreadable.
- `bron publication show` says whether the register links a document for the record. `body` is `linked` with the digest and the address it came from, or `absent`, because a record the register publishes without one has nothing to hash and an answer that merely left the provenance out would read as one whose digest went missing. `publication search` is unchanged: it names candidates and fetches no bodies.
- The appeal answer is two-valued. `unknown` meant 'this decision is not in the corpus' and there is no corpus behind this question any more: either the decision is fetched, and then its relations answer, or the fetch fails, and then it is a failure with an exit code. Consumers switching on `status` can drop that arm.
- `bron find` reads the service's envelope like every other call. A failure keeps the kind the service gave it, so `--limit 9999` exits 1 with `limit must be between 1 and 50` where it used to exit 4, and a 404 on the route no longer reads as a question that found nothing. The ranking carries the limits the service stated. Which lanes hold no vectors is now carried rather than worked out again: both were happening, and one answer stated it twice.
- A refusal from the service names the delay it asked for. Nothing writes a shared deadline for a host outside the budget, so the `Retry-After` it sent is the only thing a caller can be told, and this lane has no publisher to fall back to while it waits.
- The wetgeving lane takes no share of the shared rate-limit budget. That budget is a promise to services that rate-limit by address; the bron service states its own limit, refuses with a 429 the caller still sees, and paces nobody. `bron law show BWBR0005289 --article 162` went from 2,08-2,48 s to 0,88 s, and `bron law changes --from 2019-01-01` from 5,08 s to 1,83 s. The hold-off mattered more than the pacing: a deadline written for one unreachable host was shared by every process on the machine, and this lane has no publisher left to fall back to.
- `law changes` counts what it could not compare and says so in the `articles_not_compared` caveat: `unidentified_from` and `unidentified_to` for articles carrying no `stam-id`, one count per rendition because nothing says whether the one here is the one there, and `repeated` for stam-ids the register used more than once inside one rendition. All of them were being dropped from the list and from the counts, so a comparison that could not be made read as one that found nothing.
- `several_readings`, `reading_unresolved`, `held_text_only` and `lanes_unsearched` are read into this crate's vocabulary. They are limits the service states today, and reporting one as `unrecognised` tells a reader on the current build to upgrade.
- `src/mirror.rs` is `src/service/`, split into `client`, `model` and `search`. There is no publisher behind the address any more for a caller of this lane to be mirrored from. `BRON_MIRROR` keeps its name, which is published contract.
- `diff` compares its own `Article` rather than the shape the service sends, and reports each side as a position in the slice it came from. The rule that decides what changed does not move when the wire does, and the text compared is what bron prints rather than a field that happens to sit on a transport type.

**Fixed**

- A statutory paragraph is no longer broken in two where the publisher indented it. `xml::tidy` keeps the newlines a caller puts between blocks, and an `<al>` holding inline `<nadruk>` and `<extref>` children arrives with the file's own indentation between them, so those line breaks survived into the text. BW6 artikel 230fa onderdeel f read as two lines where the register prints one. Reading through the service settles it, because the service renders the `<al>` whole. Measured over both renditions of BW6, 340 articles, this was the one article and the one character; over the Awb's 570, none. `sources::bwb` still has it, which now reaches only what `bron update` stores.
