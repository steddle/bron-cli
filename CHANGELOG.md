# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

Nothing is tagged or published yet. Everything below can still change without a migration note, and the JSON contract is not stable until there is a `v0.1.0`.

**Added**

- `bron law`: search by any name the register gives a regulation, show a law or one article, list every published version, and diff a law between two dates. The lane reads through the bron service, which resolves a date against every manifest the register publishes and passes over renditions the publisher has withdrawn. Which rendition a date resolves to is one rule, and the service is the one place it runs.
- `BRON_MIRROR` names another deployment of the service. A value that names no service is refused at exit 1: `""`, `"/"`, `"/api/v1"`, `"example.com"` and `"/some/path"` all fail on one rule, an address with a scheme and a host.
- `law search` matches on the abbreviations and citeertitels in the register's own WTI, so `Awb` finds the Algemene wet bestuursrecht. Its window is 20 by default and 100 at most, and a cut list says so with a `truncated` caveat.
- `law show --paragraph` narrows an answer to one lid and `--item` to one item of it. A lid answer carries the lid's own byte range, Juriconnect reference and label. An item has no byte range in the corpus, so its answer stays at the lid's.
- `law show` answers `not_found` for a regulation whose text the service does not hold. The rendition and its dates still resolved; what is missing is the text, and the message names the rendition so it can still be fetched from the publisher.
- `law show` answers articles, `artikel` or `enig-artikel`. `law parts` answers every other unit a rendition prints beside them, the aanhef, a bijlage, the closing formula, each under the element the register printed it as, with its span, digest, Juriconnect reference and the images it prints. `--kind` narrows to one kind, and `--number` or, for a unit that prints no number, `--position` to one unit of it; without `--kind` it answers every unit in document order. A unit the rendition does not print exits 2 with the service's `part_not_found`.
- A unit that prints no number is answered like any other and named by its label, else by its kind and place: BWBR0020368 on 2015-01-01 prints one such article. Every field bron reads from the service is required exactly where the service's OpenAPI document requires it, so one row that leaves out an optional field never makes a whole answer unusable.
- `law versions` lists every rendition with both windows, and `held` says whether the service holds that rendition's text. `unread_because` says why a held text was not parsed into articles, `too_large`, `unparseable` or `paragraph_drift`, and a person reads it on that rendition's line.
- `law versions` and `law published` print renditions in the order the service sends them, newest first, and sort nothing.
- `law show` and `law versions` ask the service for the whole set with `limit=none`. The service pages a rendition's articles and a regulation's renditions by default, and at a terminal the whole law and the whole history are what those commands mean.
- `law changes` is the service's own comparison, read from `/changes`: the articles, and the bijlagen, aanhef and closing formula beside them, so an amended bijlage is reported where a comparison of the articles alone saw nothing. Units are matched on upstream's own identity, so a renumbered article reads as one amendment rather than a removal plus an addition. Each side is reported from its own document, with the act that produced it and the `status` and `in_force_from` its rendition prints. A unit whose words stay while its status goes from `nogniet` to `goed` is `commenced`, on the later side's `in_force_from` where the register states one. `--kind` keeps one kind of unit and `--article` the units carrying one number; the `summary` counts every unit of both renditions whatever those narrowed the list to, as the service counts it. A kind or a number neither rendition prints is a not-found. Units the register gave one stam-id twice inside a rendition are named in the `articles_not_compared` caveat and left out of the counts.
- An article the register publishes without a `stam-id` is not in an answer. The service keys articles on the publisher's own identity and holds none without one, which is what makes a comparison an identity match rather than a guess. It costs the annexes: the Awb answers with 570 articles where its XML prints 584, and the 14 missing are Bijlage 2 and Bijlage 3, the Bevoegdheidsregeling bestuursrechtspraak and the griffierecht tariffs. BWBR0005289 loses none.
- `bron find`, which answers which article, from a question or from a citation. A question is ranked over the shared corpus by the service; a term that reads as a citation is looked up, because `3:305a` put through a vector comes back as the Wft's numerically similar articles and never as artikel 3:305a BW. `data.kind` is `ranking` or `lookup`. A hit carries the `distance` of its own vector to the question, absent where the text has no vector, and a resolved citation carries none. A citation that reads more than one way is answered every way with a `several_readings` caveat, a reading that came to nothing says why in `reading_unresolved`, and a citation that resolves to nothing is a not-found, never an empty ranking.
- `--json` on `find`, `law search`, `law versions`, `law parts`, `law changes`, `law published` and `law feed` prints the envelope the service sent, byte for byte. The service's JSON is the contract, and each of these is one call to it, so nothing is read out and written back: a field or a caveat this build has no name for reaches the consumer as sent. A failure is in bron's own envelope either way.
- `bron find` puts its question in a POST body and nothing about it is written down: the material is public, the interest in it is not.
- `--as-of` and `--as-known-on`, separately or together. Geldigheid says what applied, zicht says what the register showed, and they diverge whenever something is published with retroactive effect, so a moment is a point in both windows and both coordinates go to the service. `--as-known-on` alone names that day for both. An envelope bron builds states `as_of`, and `as_known_on` where the question named one. Today is read once at startup so a run cannot straddle midnight.
- `--raw` on `bron law show`, `bron law parts` and `bron law changes`, which asks for the publisher's own bytes at every span. Without it an excerpt carries `start` and `end` and nothing else. An answer from `law show` that carries a slice says so with the `unverified_slice` caveat, read off what is in the envelope rather than off the flag: the slice is the service's word for what stands at those offsets, not a cut bron made from bytes it hashed.
- `bron case`: search decisions, narrowed with `--court` to one court by its whole name and naming the court on every hit, fetch one by ECLI, and ask what its own relations say about appeal or cassation. The appeal answer is `appealed` or `no_published_successor`, the second dated to the moment bron read the decision: it is a claim about what the register held then, not about whether an appeal was brought. A decision that cannot be fetched is a failure with an exit code, not a third value.
- `bron publication`: search and fetch kamerstukken, Staatsblad, Staatscourant and Tractatenblad. An identifier the register holds no record under is a not-found. `publication show` says whether the register links a document for the record: `body` is `linked` with the digest and the address it came from, or `absent`, because a record published without one has nothing to hash.
- Content-addressed provenance. Every document is named by the SHA-256 of its own bytes, every answer that rests on one carries that digest beside the address it came from, and articles and leden carry byte ranges into it. `provenance.frozen_by` says who froze the bytes: `bron` carries `fetched_at` and is what `case` and `publication` answer with; `service` carries no moment and is what the wetgeving lane answers with, because a reader who can see when something was fetched can see what is being worked on.
- Where bron builds the envelope, as `law show` does, caveats the service states keep the name the service gave them, because both halves state one vocabulary: `withdrawn_renditions`, `ambiguous_rendition`, `superseded_knowledge`, `retroactive`, `no_zicht_window`, `upstream_hash_absent`, `upstream_hash_differs`, `upstream_hash_rewritten`, `skipped_articles`, `ambiguous_article`, `table_flattened`, `not_commenced`, `illustration_omitted`, `truncated`, `held_text_only`, `lanes_unsearched`, `unrankable_texts`, `several_readings`, `reading_unresolved`, `citation_not_read`, `renditions_not_held`, `renditions_unread`, `articles_not_compared` and `text_outside_articles`. The three `upstream_hash_*` caveats are about the publisher's SHA-512 and never about bron's own SHA-256 digest: the manifest states none, the held bytes do not hash to the one it states, or the register rewrote the rendition after the service read it and the answer is the earlier text. One this build has no sentence for is carried whole as `unrecognised`, and printed for a person with its name and every field it came with.
- Human output of a list, `law search`, `law versions`, `law published`, `law feed`, `case search` and `publication search`, is one row per thing on stdout, and every note on those rows goes to stderr: its caveats, the legend and `law feed`'s cursor. A shell script reading stdout line by line never reads a sentence as a row.
- `--json` on every command, in a versioned envelope. `grade` is always `authority`: every source bron reads speaks with its own force, and the field is stated so a consumer never infers it.
- Exit-code contract: 0 ok, 1 usage, 2 not found, 3 network, 4 upstream, 5 local. A command line the parser will not take exits 1 rather than the argument parser's own 2, because 2 means not-found here, and with `--json` it is a `usage` envelope on stdout like any other failure. Exit 5 is bron failing at its own end: an HTTP client that would not build, a rate-limit directory that cannot be found, a stdout that will not take the answer. A failure the service states keeps its kind where the two vocabularies meet, so its `usage` exits 1 and its `not_found` exits 2; its `throttled` and `corpus` exit 4. On failure with `--json` the error is still JSON on stdout, with the human sentence on stderr.
- `error.code` on a failure the service stated: the service's own code for it, `regulation_not_found`, `rendition_withdrawn`, `unknown_parameter`, carried as sent beside the kind. It is an open string, so a code this build has never heard of crosses untouched. A failure bron states itself carries none, exit codes do not depend on it, and human output does not print it.
- Per-host rate limiting coordinated through a file lock, so processes sharing a machine share one budget against the publishers. Any language that can take an `flock(2)` can join it; see [docs/buckets.md](docs/buckets.md).
- The wetgeving lane takes no share of that budget. The budget is a promise to services that rate-limit by address; the bron service states its own limit, refuses with a 429 the caller still sees, and paces nobody. A refusal from it names the `Retry-After` it asked for.
- Nothing is kept between runs. bron writes its share of the rate-limit budget under `~/.bron/buckets` and nothing else, and `BRON_HOME` relocates it. Without a network there is no answer.
