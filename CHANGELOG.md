# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

Nothing is tagged or published yet. Everything below can still change without a migration note, and the JSON contract is not stable until there is a `v0.1.0`.

**Added**

- `bron law`: search by any name the register gives a regulation, show a law or one of its articles, show the units it prints beside them, list every published version, compare two dates, read what the register published in a window, walk what it has done since a cursor, and list the regulations that print provisions not yet applied. The lane reads through the bron service, which resolves a date against every manifest the register publishes and passes over renditions the publisher has withdrawn. Which rendition a date resolves to is one rule, and the service is the one place it runs.
- `BRON_MIRROR` names another deployment of the service. A value that names no service is refused at exit 1: `""`, `"/"`, `"/api/v1"`, `"example.com"` and `"/some/path"` all fail on one rule, an address with a scheme and a host.
- `law search` matches on the abbreviations and citeertitels in the register's own WTI, so `Awb` finds the Algemene wet bestuursrecht. Its window is 20 by default and 100 at most, and a cut list says so with a `truncated` caveat.
- `law show --paragraph` asks the service for one lid, which answers that lid's byte range, Juriconnect reference and label. `--item` cuts what a person reads down to the one item a letter opens: no route answers an item, so the span stays the lid's rather than being one nobody measured, `--json` answers the lid as the service sent it, and an item nothing opens is a not-found whichever way the answer prints.
- `law show` answers `not_found` for a rendition that answered no article, and names it: its text is not held, or it is held and was not parsed and the message says which of `too_large`, `unparseable` and `paragraph_drift` the service stated. The rendition and its dates still resolved, so it can be fetched from the publisher, and a bare 'no articles' would have a caller stop looking.
- `law show` answers articles, `artikel` or `enig-artikel`. `law parts` answers every other unit a rendition prints beside them, the aanhef, a bijlage, the closing formula, each under the element the register printed it as, with its span, digest, Juriconnect reference and the images it prints. `--kind` narrows to one kind, and `--number` or, for a unit that prints no number, `--position` to one unit of it; without `--kind` it answers every unit in document order. A unit the rendition does not print exits 2 with the service's `part_not_found`, and a `--position` or `--limit` of 0 is refused at exit 1 before it reaches the service.
- An article reads as its leden, each opened by its number; any other unit reads as the service's rendering of it, which holds the words a bijlage prints beside its leden. A unit that prints no number is answered like any other and named by its label, else by its number (with its kind where it is no article, so bijlage Ia and artikel Ia differ), else by its kind and place: BWBR0020368 on 2015-01-01 prints one such article. Every field bron reads from the service is required exactly where the service's OpenAPI document requires it, so one row that leaves out an optional field never makes a whole answer unusable.
- Every name the routes declare can now be sent. `law published --offset` and `law search --offset` read past a list the service cut at its window; `law search --type` keeps one kind of regulation and `--as-of`/`--as-known-on` ask which names the register stated on a day; `law changes --as-known-on` compares two days as the register showed them then; `law parts --paragraph` narrows a unit to one lid; and `find --regulation` puts a question to one law or several, refusing what is no BWB identifier before it is asked. `law show` sends no `offset`, because it asks for the whole law with `limit=none` and there is no page to skip.
- `law about <ID>`, which answers what the index knows about one regulation: its type, how much of it the corpus means to hold and how many of its renditions it holds, the body the register names, where the register files it, and the publications that created it.
- `law amendments <ID>`, which answers the texts the act that established a regulation produced elsewhere, one row per text with the regulation it stands in, what it is, what it did and the day it applies from. The act itself is a note on the rows and goes to stderr. It is drawn from the regulations whose text the corpus holds, which the `held_text_only` caveat states.
- `law relations <ID>`, which answers what the register states rests on a law and what cites it: one row per relation with its kind, the article it is stated for where the register names one, the regulation it points at and that relation's window. `--kind` keeps one kind and `--article` one article, as the service matches it. A law the register states no relation for says so in words.
- `law upcoming`, which answers the regulations whose rendition in force prints provisions it does not yet apply, one row per regulation in the service's order: the first day to come those provisions state, or `no day yet`, the rendition read, how many wait and, where not all do, how many state a day to come, the title, and the acts that produced them and the besluiten that commence them. `--from` and `--to` bound the days to come, and a window that runs backwards is refused at exit 1 before it is asked; `--type`, `--as-of`, `--as-known-on`, `--limit` and `--offset` go to the service as named. Treaties are left out and counted in `treaties_left_out`, and `not_in_bwb` says an act the BWB does not print yet is missing. Nothing waiting is exit 0.
- `law history <ID> --article N`, which answers every text one article has had, from the renditions the corpus holds: one row per text, with the window it applied in, the act that produced it and which renditions carry it. `--kind bijlage` asks for a unit the register prints no article number for, and `--as-known-on` reads the history as the register showed it on a day. A window whose renditions are not held reads as `not held`, and one whose held renditions carry no such unit as `not carried`, because silence would read as a window the article said nothing in.
- `law versions` lists every rendition with both windows, and `held` says whether the service holds that rendition's text. `unread_because` says why a held text was not parsed into articles: `too_large`, `unparseable` or `paragraph_drift`.
- `law versions` and `law published` print renditions in the order the service sends them, newest first, and sort nothing.
- `law show` and `law versions` ask the service for the whole set with `limit=none`. `law parts` sends the page `--limit` and `--offset` name, because one bijlage can run to a hundred kilobytes, and `/changes` does not page. The service pages a rendition's articles and a regulation's renditions by default, and at a terminal the whole law and the whole history are what those commands mean.
- `law changes` is the service's own comparison, read from `/changes`: the articles, and the bijlagen, aanhef and closing formula beside them, so an amended bijlage is reported beside the articles. Units are matched on upstream's own identity, so a renumbered article reads as one amendment rather than a removal plus an addition. Each side is reported from its own document, with the act that produced it and the `status` and `in_force_from` its rendition prints. A unit whose words stay while its status goes from `nogniet` to `goed` is `commenced`, on the later side's `in_force_from` where the register states one. `--kind` keeps one kind of unit and `--article` the units carrying one number; the `summary` counts every unit of both renditions whatever those narrowed the list to, as the service counts it, and a person reading a narrowed list reads the counts as the whole regulation's. A kind or a number neither rendition prints is a not-found. Units the register gave one stam-id twice inside a rendition are named in the `articles_not_compared` caveat and left out of the counts.
- An article the register publishes without a `stam-id` is not in an answer. The service keys articles on the publisher's own identity and holds none without one, which is what makes a comparison an identity match rather than a guess. It costs the annexes, and `law parts` does not answer them either: the Awb answers with 570 articles where its XML prints 584, and the 14 missing are Bijlage 2 and Bijlage 3, the Bevoegdheidsregeling bestuursrechtspraak and the griffierecht tariffs. BWBR0005289 loses none.
- `bron find`, which answers which article, from a question or from a citation. A question is ranked over the shared corpus by the service; a term that reads as a citation is looked up, because `3:305a` put through a vector comes back as the Wft's numerically similar articles and never as artikel 3:305a BW. `data.kind` is `ranking` or `lookup`. A hit carries the `distance` of its own vector to the question, absent where the text has no vector, and a resolved citation carries none. A citation that reads more than one way is answered every way with a `several_readings` caveat, a reading that came to nothing says why in `reading_unresolved`, and a citation that resolves to nothing is a not-found, never an empty ranking.
- The wetgeving lane answers what the service answers, in the service's own words: `regulation_id` rather than `law_id`, the rendition under `expression.label`, each unit's byte range as `span` with the publisher's bytes beside it as `raw` under `--raw`, and its leden as `paragraphs`, each carrying its own `number`. The text of a unit is the service's rendering of it, so lid numbers stand in `paragraphs[].number` rather than inside the text, and a repealed article's empty lid is in `paragraphs` like any other.
- `--json` on every command that reads the service prints the envelope the service sent, byte for byte: `find`, `law search`, `law show`, `law parts`, `law versions`, `law changes`, `law published`, `law feed` and `law upcoming`. The service's JSON is the contract, and each of these is one call to it, so nothing is read out and written back: a field or a caveat this build has no name for reaches the consumer as sent, under the name the service gave it. `case` and `publication` read a publisher rather than the service, and bron defines their answers in `wire.rs`. A failure is in bron's own envelope either way.
- `bron find` puts its question in a POST body and nothing about it is written down: the material is public, the interest in it is not.
- `--as-of` and `--as-known-on`, separately or together. Geldigheid says what applied, zicht says what the register showed, and they diverge whenever something is published with retroactive effect, so a moment is a point in both windows and both coordinates go to the service. `--as-known-on` alone names that day for both. An envelope bron builds states `as_of`, and `as_known_on` where the question named one. Today is read once at startup so a run cannot straddle midnight.
- `--raw` on `bron law show`, `bron law parts` and `bron law changes`, which asks for the publisher's own bytes at every span. Without it a span carries `start` and `end` and nothing else. Only `--json` carries the slice; a person reads the service's reading either way. That slice is the service's word for what stands at those offsets rather than a cut bron made from bytes it hashed, which is what fetching the blob and cutting it again settles.
- `bron case`: search decisions, narrowed with `--court` to one court by its whole name and naming the court on every hit, fetch one by ECLI, and ask what its own relations say about appeal or cassation. The appeal answer is `appealed` or `no_published_successor`, the second dated to the moment bron read the decision: it is a claim about what the register held then, not about whether an appeal was brought. A decision that cannot be fetched is a failure with an exit code, not a third value.
- `bron publication`: search and fetch kamerstukken, Staatsblad, Staatscourant and Tractatenblad. An identifier the register holds no record under is a not-found. `publication show` says whether the register links a document for the record: `body` is `linked` with the digest and the address it came from, or `absent`, because a record published without one has nothing to hash.
- `bron blob <digest>`, which fetches the document a digest names, hashes the bytes as they arrive, and hands them over on stdout untouched, so `bron blob <digest> | shasum -a 256` prints the digest back. Bytes that hash to anything else are refused at exit 4 and none of them reach stdout. `--span START..END` cuts the range an answer printed, out of bytes bron hashed itself rather than on the service's word. What was checked goes to stderr, and `--json` answers it as provenance with the text where the bytes are text. A digest that is not 64 lowercase hex characters is refused before the request; one the service holds nothing under is its `blob_not_found`, exit 2.
- Content-addressed provenance. Every document is named by the SHA-256 of its own bytes, every answer that rests on one carries that digest, and every unit of text carries a byte range into it. The address is there where bron fetched the bytes itself, `case` and `publication`; in the wetgeving lane the service holds them, and a reader fetches them from it by the digest. In the wetgeving lane the service froze the document and names it by its digest, so a reader fetches those bytes and cuts the span again. `case` and `publication` carry `provenance.frozen_by: bron`, with the `fetched_at` of the moment bron read and hashed them itself.
- Caveats keep the name the service gave them, because both halves state one vocabulary: `withdrawn_renditions`, `ambiguous_rendition`, `superseded_knowledge`, `retroactive`, `no_zicht_window`, `upstream_hash_absent`, `upstream_hash_differs`, `upstream_hash_rewritten`, `skipped_articles`, `ambiguous_article`, `table_flattened`, `not_commenced`, `illustration_omitted`, `truncated`, `held_text_only`, `lanes_unsearched`, `unrankable_texts`, `several_readings`, `reading_unresolved`, `citation_not_read`, `renditions_not_held`, `renditions_unread`, `articles_not_compared`, `texts_unindexed`, `words_unsearched`, `translation_left_out`, `text_outside_articles`, `read_for_articles`, `treaties_left_out` and `not_in_bwb`. A citation read more than one way names the regulations that share the name where the register gives it to more than one. A question says which of its words full text did not search, and a treaty that prints one number in its authentic text and in a translation says so, because the authentic text is what answered. The three `upstream_hash_*` caveats are about the publisher's SHA-512 and never about bron's own SHA-256 digest: the manifest states none, the held bytes do not hash to the one it states, or the register rewrote the rendition after the service read it and the answer is the earlier text. One this build has no sentence for is carried whole as `unrecognised`, and printed for a person with its name and every field it came with.
- Human output of a list, `law search`, `law versions`, `law published`, `law feed`, `law upcoming`, `case search` and `publication search`, is one row per thing on stdout, and every note on those rows goes to stderr: its caveats, the legend and `law feed`'s cursor. A shell script reading stdout line by line never reads a sentence as a row.
- What a person reads states what the service states. A rendition reads as one row wherever it is listed, `law versions` and `law published` alike: whether its text is held, its geldigheid and zicht windows (`-` where the manifest states no zicht dates), what the publisher's hash says of the held bytes, how many images a held rendition prints, `withdrawn`, why a held text was not parsed, and the name the register gave the regulation that day; `law published` names the regulation too. A `law feed` event that names a rendition carries its two windows and `withdrawn`. A `law search` row says the regulation's type, how much of it the corpus means to hold and how many of its renditions the corpus holds.
- `law show` and `law parts` print a unit alike: under the moment, the rendition that answered and its window; the unit's language and whether that text is authentic, where it stands in a treaty; the source digest and span with the address to fetch it from; the Juriconnect reference; the images it prints; and whether it applies, through which act. `law changes` names each side's rendition, window, hash state and address, and under each unit its path, the act on each side and its span in each document.
- A `find` hit names the rendition it was found in, whether the vector or full text found it, and the other places the same words sit, the article's own lid among them, counting those it does not name. A lookup says the lid and the name it read, and which `law show` answers that lid, because the service reads a lid and does not look it up.
- `case show` prints the decision's full text after the summary, when it was published, its procedure and the address it was fetched from. A `case search` hit names its court and the day it was decided, and a publication the day it was published.
- `--json` on every command, in a versioned envelope. `grade` is always `authority`: every source bron reads speaks with its own force, and the field is stated so a consumer never infers it.
- Exit-code contract: 0 ok, 1 usage, 2 not found, 3 network, 4 upstream, 5 local. A command line the parser will not take exits 1 rather than the argument parser's own 2, because 2 means not-found here, and with `--json` it is a `usage` envelope on stdout like any other failure. Exit 5 is bron failing at its own end: an HTTP client that would not build, a rate-limit directory that cannot be found, a stdout that will not take the answer. A failure the service states keeps its kind where the two vocabularies meet, so its `usage` exits 1 and its `not_found` exits 2; its `throttled`, `corpus` and `source` exit 4, where it prints as 'the service could not answer: …' rather than as an unusable response, because the answer was read. Under exit 4 a stated `corpus` code such as `rendition_too_large` is a fact about that rendition and not a reason to retry. A 502, 503 or 504 that carries no envelope is named for its status and never called an unusable answer: a 504 reads as the service, or something in front of it, not answering in time, to be asked again later or narrowed. bron asks once and does not retry. On failure with `--json` the error is still JSON on stdout, with the human sentence on stderr.
- `error.code` on a failure the service stated: the service's own code for it, `regulation_not_found`, `rendition_withdrawn`, `unknown_parameter`, carried as sent beside the kind. A 429 or a 503 is read off the status, and where its body is the envelope the code and the sentence the service stated travel with the delay it named, so `fetch_budget_spent`, a budget every caller shares, is not read as the caller's own limit. It is an open string, so a code this build has never heard of crosses untouched. A failure bron states itself carries none, exit codes do not depend on it, and human output does not print it.
- A `Retry-After` is read as the seconds or the date RFC 9110 allows, so a host that named a wait is never reported as having named none, and a date already past asks for no wait at all.
- Per-host rate limiting coordinated through a file lock, so processes sharing a machine share one budget against the publishers. Any language that can take an `flock(2)` can join it; see [docs/buckets.md](docs/buckets.md).
- The wetgeving lane takes no share of that budget. The budget is a promise to services that rate-limit by address; the bron service states its own limit, refuses with a 429 the caller still sees, and paces nobody. A refusal from it names the `Retry-After` it asked for.
- Nothing is kept between runs. bron writes its share of the rate-limit budget under `~/.bron/buckets` and nothing else, and `BRON_HOME` relocates it. Without a network there is no answer.
