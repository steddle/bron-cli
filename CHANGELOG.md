# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

Nothing is tagged or published yet. Everything below can still change without a migration note, and the JSON contract is not stable until there is a `v0.1.0`.

**Added**

- `bron law`: search by any name the register gives a regulation, show a law or one of its articles, show the units it prints beside them, list every published version, compare two dates, read what the register published in a window, walk what it has done since a cursor, and list the regulations that print provisions not yet applied. The lane reads through the bron service, which resolves a date against every manifest the register publishes and passes over renditions the publisher has withdrawn. Which rendition a date resolves to is one rule, and the service is the one place it runs.
- `BRON_MIRROR` names another deployment of the service. A value that names no service is refused at exit 1: `""`, `"/"`, `"/api/v1"`, `"example.com"` and `"/some/path"` all fail on one rule, an address with a scheme and a host.
- `law search` matches on the abbreviations and citeertitels in the register's own WTI, so `Awb` finds the Algemene wet bestuursrecht. Its window is 20 by default and 100 at most, and a cut list says so with a `truncated` caveat.
- `law show --paragraph` asks the service for one lid, of `--article` or, without it, of every article that prints one, as the API and the MCP answer it; each carries that lid's byte range, Juriconnect reference and label. `--item` cuts what a person reads down to the one item a letter opens: no route answers an item, so the span stays the lid's rather than being one nobody measured, and `--json` answers the lid as the service sent it. Where a lid prints no such item, a person reads that it does not.
- `law show` for a rendition that answers no article is the service's answer, exit 0: `--json` is the empty list as sent, and a person reads one line naming the rendition and why. Its text is not held; it is held and was not parsed, with the `too_large`, `unparseable` or `paragraph_drift` the service stated; or it prints no article, as asked, and `law parts` answers the other units it prints. The rendition and its dates still resolved, so it can be fetched from the publisher.
- `law show` answers articles, `artikel` or `enig-artikel`. `law parts` answers every other unit a rendition prints beside them, the aanhef, a bijlage, the closing formula, each under the element the register printed it as, with its span, digest, Juriconnect reference and the images it prints. `--kind` narrows to one kind, and `--number` or, for a unit that prints no number, `--position` to one unit of it; without `--kind` it answers every unit in document order. `--not-commenced` keeps the units the rendition publishes and does not yet apply, of every kind, as `law show --not-commenced` does for articles: a rendition with none exits 2 with `nothing_not_commenced`, and one read for its articles alone exits 4 with `rendition_read_for_articles`. A unit the rendition does not print exits 2 with the service's `part_not_found`, and a `--number` or `--position` without `--kind`, both at once, or a `--position` or `--limit` of 0 is the service's `invalid_parameter`, exit 1.
- An article reads as its leden, each opened by its number; any other unit reads as the service's rendering of it, which holds the words a bijlage prints beside its leden. A unit that prints no number is answered like any other and named by its label, else by its number (with its kind where it is no article, so bijlage Ia and artikel Ia differ), else by its kind and place: BWBR0020368 on 2015-01-01 prints one such article. Every field bron reads from the service is required exactly where the service's OpenAPI document requires it, so one row that leaves out an optional field never makes a whole answer unusable.
- Every name the routes declare can now be sent. `law published --offset` and `law search --offset` read past a list the service cut at its window; `law search --type` keeps one kind of regulation and `--as-of`/`--as-known-on` ask which names the register stated on a day; `law changes --as-known-on` compares two days as the register showed them then; `law parts --paragraph` narrows a unit to one lid; and `find --regulation` puts a question to one law or several. `law show` sends no `offset`, because it asks for the whole law with `limit=none` and there is no page to skip.
- `law about <ID>`, which answers what the index knows about one regulation: its type, how much of it the corpus means to hold and how many of its renditions it holds, the body the register names, where the register files it, and the publications that created it.
- `law amendments <ID>`, which answers the texts the act that established a regulation produced elsewhere, one row per text with the regulation it stands in, what it is, what it did and the day it applies from. The act itself is a note on the rows and goes to stderr. It is drawn from the regulations whose text the corpus holds, which the `held_text_only` caveat states.
- `law relations <ID>`, which answers what the register states rests on a law and what cites it: one row per relation with its kind, the article it is stated for where the register names one, the regulation it points at and that relation's window. `--kind` keeps one kind and `--article` one article, as the service matches it. A law the register states no relation for says so in words.
- `law upcoming`, which answers the regulations whose rendition in force prints provisions it does not yet apply, one row per regulation in the service's order: the first day to come those provisions state, or `no day yet`, the rendition read, how many wait and, where some do and some do not, how many state a day to come, the title, and the acts that produced them and the besluiten that commence them. `--from` and `--to` bound the days to come, and a window that runs backwards is the service's `reversed_window`, exit 1; `--type`, `--as-of`, `--as-known-on`, `--limit` and `--offset` go to the service as named. Treaties are left out and counted in `treaties_left_out`, and `not_in_bwb` says an act the BWB does not print yet is missing. `decrees_unmatched` counts the held besluiten that link to no act, so bron cannot say what they commence, and names those published in the last 15 days. Nothing waiting is exit 0.
- `law history <ID> --article N`, which answers every text one article has had, from the renditions the corpus holds: one row per text, with the window it applied in, the act that produced it and which renditions carry it. `--kind bijlage` asks for a unit the register prints no article number for, and `--as-known-on` reads the history as the register showed it on a day. A window whose renditions are not held reads as `not held`, and one whose held renditions carry no such unit as `not carried`, because silence would read as a window the article said nothing in.
- `law versions` lists every rendition with both windows, and `held` says whether the service holds that rendition's text. `unread_because` says why a held text was not parsed into articles: `too_large`, `unparseable` or `paragraph_drift`.
- `law versions` and `law published` print renditions in the order the service sends them, newest first, and sort nothing.
- `law show` and `law versions` ask the service for the whole set with `limit=none`. `law parts` sends the page `--limit` and `--offset` name, because one bijlage can run to a hundred kilobytes, and `/changes` does not page. The service pages a rendition's articles and a regulation's renditions by default, and at a terminal the whole law and the whole history are what those commands mean.
- `law changes` is the service's own comparison, read from `/changes`: the articles, and the bijlagen, aanhef and closing formula beside them, so an amended bijlage is reported beside the articles. Units are matched on upstream's own identity, so a renumbered article reads as one amendment rather than a removal plus an addition. Each side is reported from its own document, with the act that produced it and the `status` and `in_force_from` its rendition prints. A unit whose words stay while its status goes from `nogniet` to `goed` is `commenced`, on the later side's `in_force_from` where the register states one. `--kind` keeps one kind of unit and `--article` the units carrying one number; the `summary` counts every unit of both renditions whatever those narrowed the list to, as the service counts it, and a person reading a narrowed list reads the counts as the whole regulation's. A kind or a number neither rendition prints is a not-found. Units the register gave one stam-id twice inside a rendition are named in the `articles_not_compared` caveat and left out of the counts.
- An article the register publishes without a `stam-id` is not in an answer. The service keys articles on the publisher's own identity and holds none without one, which is what makes a comparison an identity match rather than a guess. It costs the annexes, and `law parts` does not answer them either: the Awb answers with 570 articles where its XML prints 584, and the 14 missing are Bijlage 2 and Bijlage 3, the Bevoegdheidsregeling bestuursrechtspraak and the griffierecht tariffs. BWBR0005289 loses none.
- `bron find`, which answers which article, from a question or from a citation. A question is ranked over the shared corpus by the service; a term that reads as a citation is looked up, because `3:305a` put through a vector comes back as the Wft's numerically similar articles and never as artikel 3:305a BW. `data.kind` is `ranking` or `lookup`. A hit carries the `distance` of its own vector to the question, absent where the text has no vector, and a resolved citation carries none. A citation that reads more than one way is answered every way with a `several_readings` caveat, a reading that came to nothing says why in `reading_unresolved`, and a citation that resolves to nothing is a not-found, never an empty ranking.
- The wetgeving lane answers what the service answers, in the service's own words: `regulation_id` rather than `law_id`, the rendition under `expression.label`, each unit's byte range as `span` with the publisher's bytes beside it as `raw` under `--raw`, and its leden as `paragraphs`, each carrying its own `number`. The text of a unit is the service's rendering of it, so lid numbers stand in `paragraphs[].number` rather than inside the text, and a repealed article's empty lid is in `paragraphs` like any other.
- `--json` on every command that reads the service prints the envelope the service sent, byte for byte: `find`, `law search`, `law about`, `law show`, `law parts`, `law versions`, `law history`, `law changes`, `law amendments`, `law relations`, `law published`, `law feed`, `law upcoming`, `publication search`, `publication show` and `dossier show`. The service's JSON is the contract, and each of these is one call to it, so nothing is read out and written back: a field or a caveat this build has no name for reaches the consumer as sent, under the name the service gave it. `case` reads rechtspraak.nl rather than the service, and bron defines its answers in `wire.rs`. A failure is in bron's own envelope either way.
- `bron find` puts its question in a POST body and nothing about it is written down: the material is public, the interest in it is not.
- `--as-of` and `--as-known-on`, separately or together. Geldigheid says what applied, zicht says what the register showed, and they diverge whenever something is published with retroactive effect, so a moment is a point in both windows. Each goes to the service exactly as typed and is left out where its flag was: the service reads `--as-known-on` alone as that day for both coordinates and a moment named by neither as today, and its answer states the moment it resolved, which is the one a person reads above it. No clock on this machine decides which text answers, and `law changes` without `--to` is the service's today too.
- `--raw` on `bron law show`, `bron law parts` and `bron law changes`, which asks for the publisher's own bytes at every span. Without it a span carries `start` and `end` and nothing else. Only `--json` carries the slice; a person reads the service's reading either way. That slice is the service's word for what stands at those offsets rather than a cut bron made from bytes it hashed, which is what fetching the blob and cutting it again settles.
- `bron case`: search decisions, narrowed with `--court` to one court by its whole name and naming the court on every hit, fetch one by ECLI, and ask what its own relations say about appeal or cassation. The appeal answer is `appealed` or `no_published_successor`, the second dated to the moment bron read the decision: it is a claim about what the register held then, not about whether an appeal was brought. A decision that cannot be fetched is a failure with an exit code, not a third value.
- `bron publication` reads the bron service rather than the publisher directly: kamerstukken, Staatsblad, Staatscourant, Tractatenblad and bijlagen. `publication show` answers a publication held by the corpus, one fetched on demand and passed on unkept under a `publication_not_held` caveat, or a refusal, `invalid_publication_id` for a malformed one and `publication_outside_scope` for one bron is known to keep out; an identifier the register holds nothing under is `publication_not_found`. A held or passed-through answer names its manifestations under the digests that name them, the dossier it states as its own, and, where a manifestation is XML, the articles it prints at their spans, with `--rendered` and `--raw` as `law show` takes them. `publication search` posts the same question the service posts to the publisher and answers its `source_total` and `next`, narrowed by `--collection`, sent exactly as typed by the service's own code (`kst`, `stb`, `stcrt`, `trb`, `blg`, `h` for the Handelingen) so the service says which it takes, and `--dossier`; `--next` walks a search to its end.
- `bron dossier show <number>`, which answers one kamerstukdossier that the act behind a held text names: the bill's title, the acts that name it with the regulations they produced, and its papers oldest first, one row each with its identifier, date, kind as the service reads it off the paper's own title and, on an amendement or a motie, every besluit the Tweede Kamer states for it with the digest of the answer that states it. `--kind`, `--limit` and `--offset` go to the service as typed. A dossier whose acts all predate 1995 answers no paper under `papers_before_digital`, and an amendement or motie the Tweede Kamer states no besluit for is counted in `fate_unknown`, which is not a vote against. A dossier no held text names is the service's `dossier_not_named`, exit 2, and a value that is no dossier number its `invalid_dossier_number`, exit 1. The rows go to stdout, and the head, the acts and every note to stderr.
- `law show` prints under an article the passages of the memorie van toelichting and notas van wijziging the service read as explaining it, each as bron's reading: the paper's citation, the digest and byte range it rests on, the bill's formula it went through, and whether the act prints that place of the bill otherwise. `--json` carries them as `passages`, as sent.
- `bron blob <digest>`, which fetches the document a digest names, hashes the bytes as they arrive, and hands them over on stdout untouched, so `bron blob <digest> | shasum -a 256` prints the digest back. Bytes that hash to anything else are refused at exit 4 and none of them reach stdout. `--span START..END` cuts the range an answer printed, out of bytes bron hashed itself rather than on the service's word. What was checked goes to stderr, and `--json` answers it as provenance with the text where the bytes are text. A digest that is not 64 lowercase hex characters is the service's `invalid_digest`, exit 1, and one it holds nothing under is its `blob_not_found`, exit 2.
- Content-addressed provenance. Every document is named by the SHA-256 of its own bytes, every answer that rests on one carries that digest, and every unit of text carries a byte range into it. The address is there where bron fetched the bytes itself, `case`; the service holds the rest, the legislation and the publications, and names each by its digest, so a reader fetches those bytes from it and cuts the span again. `case` carries `provenance.frozen_by: bron`, with the `fetched_at` of the moment bron read and hashed the decision itself.
- Caveats keep the name the service gave them, because both halves state one vocabulary: `withdrawn_renditions`, `ambiguous_rendition`, `superseded_knowledge`, `retroactive`, `no_zicht_window`, `upstream_hash_absent`, `upstream_hash_differs`, `upstream_hash_rewritten`, `skipped_articles`, `ambiguous_article`, `table_flattened`, `not_commenced`, `illustration_omitted`, `truncated`, `held_text_only`, `lanes_unsearched`, `unrankable_texts`, `several_readings`, `reading_unresolved`, `citation_not_read`, `renditions_not_held`, `renditions_unread`, `articles_not_compared`, `texts_unindexed`, `words_unsearched`, `translation_left_out`, `text_outside_articles`, `read_for_articles`, `treaties_left_out`, `not_in_bwb`, `units_withdrawn_by_remark`, `ranking_short`, `counts_not_taken`, `cursor_ahead`, `places_read`, `index_behind`, `acts_left_out`, `founding_acts_unread`, `decrees_unmatched`, `commencement_pending`, `publication_not_held`, `source_total_estimated`, `papers_before_digital`, `fate_unknown`, `provenance_of_article`, `papers_not_held`, `publications_left_out`, `machine_reading`, `passage_not_read` and `explains_earlier_text`. `provenance_of_article` says which fields of an answer narrowed to one lid describe the whole article, because the register records provenance per article. `papers_not_held` says how many of the papers the publisher lists for a dossier the answer leaves out while bron reads them, and `publications_left_out` how many hits on a page of `publication search` are of a kind bron does not open, so a short or empty page is not the end: its `next` walks on. A citation read more than one way names the regulations that share the name where the register gives it to more than one. A question says which of its words full text did not search, and a treaty that prints one number in its authentic text and in a translation says so, because the authentic text is what answered. The three `upstream_hash_*` caveats are about the publisher's SHA-512 and never about bron's own SHA-256 digest: the manifest states none, the held bytes do not hash to the one it states, or the register rewrote the rendition after the service read it and the answer is the earlier text. One this build has no sentence for is carried whole as `unrecognised`, and printed for a person with its name and every field it came with.
- Human output of a list, `law search`, `law versions`, `law published`, `law feed`, `law upcoming`, `case search`, `publication search` and `dossier show`, is one row per thing on stdout, and every note on those rows goes to stderr: its caveats, the legend and `law feed`'s cursor. A shell script reading stdout line by line never reads a sentence as a row.
- What a person reads states what the service states. A rendition reads as one row wherever it is listed, `law versions` and `law published` alike: whether its text is held, its geldigheid and zicht windows (`-` where the manifest states no zicht dates), what the publisher's hash says of the held bytes, how many images a held rendition prints, `withdrawn`, why a held text was not parsed, and the name the register gave the regulation that day; `law published` names the regulation too. A `law feed` event that names a rendition carries its two windows and `withdrawn`. A `law search` row says the regulation's type, how much of it the corpus means to hold and how many of its renditions the corpus holds.
- `law show` and `law parts` print a unit alike: under the moment, the rendition that answered and its window; the unit's language and whether that text is authentic, where it stands in a treaty; the source digest and span with the address to fetch it from; the Juriconnect reference; the images it prints; and whether it applies, through which act. `law changes` names each side's rendition, window, hash state and address, and under each unit its path, the act on each side and its span in each document.
- A unit's editorial remarks, the register's own words in `<opmerkingen-inhoud>`, are printed verbatim with their byte range wherever the service answers them: `law show`, `law parts`, each side of `law changes`, and on stderr beside the rows of `law history`. A unit printed `nogniet` whose remark carries the register's fixed sentence for a change that never took effect reads as 'the register states this change will not take effect' and is published by its act rather than waiting on it; `law history` marks its row `withdrawn`. The service leaves such a unit out of `not_commenced` and counts it in `units_withdrawn_by_remark`, and bron prints both notes as the service stated them. `--json` carries `remarks` and `withdrawn` as the service sent them.
- A `find` answer names each unit once, where its best text ranked, and its other leden that ranked stand under it as `same unit` rows in rank order, after the unit's own other places, each with its own distance, the lists that found it and the other places its text sits. `--json` carries them as `leden`, as the service sent them.
- `law changes` says of a lid the later rendition prints under another number with the same words 'lid N was lid M, word for word', from the service's `renumbered_from`.
- A `find` hit names the rendition it was found in, whether the vector or full text found it, and the other places the same words sit, the article's own lid among them, counting those it does not name. A lookup says the lid and the name it read, and which `law show` answers that lid, because the service reads a lid and does not look it up.
- `case show` prints the decision's full text after the summary, when it was published, its procedure and the address it was fetched from. A `case search` hit names its court and the day it was decided, and a publication the day it was published.
- `--json` on every command, in a versioned envelope. `grade` is always `authority`: every source bron reads speaks with its own force, and the field is stated so a consumer never infers it.
- Exit-code contract: 0 ok, 1 usage, 2 not found, 3 network, 4 upstream, 5 local. A command line the parser will not take exits 1 rather than the argument parser's own 2, because 2 means not-found here, and with `--json` it is a `usage` envelope on stdout like any other failure. Exit 5 is bron failing at its own end: an HTTP client that would not build, a rate-limit directory that cannot be found, a stdout that will not take the answer. A failure the service states keeps its kind where the two vocabularies meet, so its `usage` exits 1 and its `not_found` exits 2; its `throttled`, `corpus` and `source` exit 4, and `--json` carries each under the kind the service gave it, where a person reads 'the service could not answer: …' rather than an unusable response, because the answer was read. `upstream` is the kind of a failure bron states itself: a 502, 503 or 504 with no envelope, or an answer it could not read. Under exit 4 a stated `corpus` code such as `rendition_too_large` is a fact about that rendition and not a reason to retry. A 502, 503 or 504 that carries no envelope is named for its status and never called an unusable answer: a 504 reads as the service, or something in front of it, not answering in time, to be asked again later or narrowed. bron asks once and does not retry. On failure with `--json` the error is still JSON on stdout, with the human sentence on stderr.
- A question goes to the service as asked, and where the service refuses it the refusal is the service's, with the kind, code and message it states, as the API and the MCP give it: `law published` without a window is `missing_parameter` and one that runs backwards `reversed_window`, as are `law upcoming` and `law changes` with days that run backwards; `law feed` without `--since` and `law changes` without `--from` are `missing_parameter`; a `--limit` of 0 or -1, a `--since` or `--position` of -1 and a date that names no day, `--as-of 2026-02-30` or `--from gisteren`, are `invalid_parameter`; `find` without a question is `missing_parameter`. Every date, `--limit`, `--offset`, `--since` and `--position` on a command that reads the service is sent as typed, and a negative number is the value of the flag before it. A BWB identifier goes out as typed, one path segment, so `bwbr0005289` is answered as the service answers it in any case and `Awb` is its `invalid_regulation_id`. bron refuses only what it alone can judge: a `case search` `--limit` that is no whole number or `--from` that is no date, because rechtspraak.nl is read directly and bron is its only judge, a missing argument, `--item` without `--paragraph`, a `BRON_MIRROR` that names no service, and a `--span` outside the bytes it hashed.
- A failure the service stated reads in `--json` as the service stated it: its error object as it arrived, so its kind, `throttled` as the API and the MCP give it and never `upstream`, `error.code`, the service's own code for it, `regulation_not_found`, `rendition_withdrawn`, `unknown_parameter`, `message` and `detail` as sent, the one never folded into the other, and any field the service adds later, so the labels of the withdrawn renditions covering a date are `detail` and not a clause of the message. A person reads the message and then each line of `detail` on stderr. A failure bron states itself, `network` or `local`, carries its own sentence and cause chain as `message` and `detail`. A 429 or a 503 is read off the status. Its delay is the body's `retry_after` where the service states one, and crosses with the error object; where it states none and its `Retry-After` header named one, bron writes that as `retry_after`, in seconds, after the service's own fields, so the delay is written once. A 429 or a 503 with no envelope carries it as `retry_after` in bron's own. A person reads it in the sentence either way, so `fetch_budget_spent`, a budget every caller shares, is not read as the caller's own limit. It is an open string, so a code this build has never heard of crosses untouched. A failure bron states itself carries none, exit codes do not depend on it, and human output does not print it.
- A `Retry-After` is read as the seconds or the date RFC 9110 allows, so a host that named a wait is never reported as having named none, and a date already past asks for no wait at all.
- Per-host rate limiting coordinated through a file lock, so processes sharing a machine share one budget against the publishers. Any language that can take an `flock(2)` can join it; see [docs/buckets.md](docs/buckets.md).
- The wetgeving lane takes no share of that budget. The budget is a promise to services that rate-limit by address; the bron service states its own limit, refuses with a 429 the caller still sees, and paces nobody. A refusal from it names the `Retry-After` it asked for.
- Nothing is kept between runs. bron writes its share of the rate-limit budget under `~/.bron/buckets` and nothing else, and `BRON_HOME` relocates it. Without a network there is no answer.

**Changed**

- `publication search --collection` takes the service's codes alone and sends them as typed. The word forms `kamerstuk`, `staatsblad`, `staatscourant` and `tractatenblad` are gone: typed, each reaches the service and is its `invalid_parameter`, exit 1, as the API and the MCP answer it.
- Every command that reads the service sends `--limit` and `--offset` only where typed, so the service's own window is the default and a bare question answers through the CLI as it does through the API and the MCP. Three windows a person sees change: `find` answers 10 hits rather than 20, and `law amendments` and `law relations` 100 rows rather than 50. `law show` and `law versions` still ask for the whole set, and `case search` keeps its own 20.
- `publication search` without `--collection` now searches every collection the service reads: kamerstukken, the Staatsblad, the Staatscourant, the Tractatenblad and bijlagen. It previously searched kamerstukken only, because the old CQL client needed one collection to query and defaulted to that one.
- `publication search` with no query, `--collection` and `--dossier` is now the service's `missing_parameter`, exit 1. It previously ran an unscoped search of the register's kamerstukken, because the old client had a query to send either way.
